## Introduction
It's a widely considered notion amongst humans that we are the most intelligent species on this planet. If someones goes to the extent of validating and question the reasoning, many of us would come up with achievements like humans are the only species that came up with the idea of language, society, harnessing resources on the planet, technological innovations etc. 

## The Notion
If we pursue the notion, it does make sense. Let's think if there are there any other species on this planet who have been able to built trains or communication technology ? Did any species learn how to cook their food ? Did they try to understand what lies beyond our planet ? Did they ever try to figure out what's inside them ? Did they pursue spirituality ?

We humans have always been curious and adaptive. We adapted to our surroundings, inculcated resourcefulness and harnessed environment to our leverage and progressed to the stage where we are now. We have developed language to communicate. We have been able to travel to our moon, mars and sent [Voyager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyager_1) to explore the vast limits of the universe way beyond our solar system. We are building quantum computers to harness the potential of parallel universes. 

Which species on earth can beat this ? 

**We surely are the epitome of intelligence on this planet!**

## What about the questions ?
Let's take a moment and think deeply!

Has any other species challenged our claims so far or it's just us who shouted in the [echo chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Echo_chamber_(media)) and stamped the assumption as the fact ? Could it be possible that other species stand a chance in the comparison or we are just unbeatable ?

Let's talk about the plants. The species that can't even move by itself and doesn't either show prominent signs if they are even living being. It's also evident they haven't developed any sort of technology or communication. But the deeper question is, do they really need to ?

If we really need to find the absolute truth, we would have to get rid of our internal biases. We will need to drop the criteria we think is most suited to compare two species and try to question every fundamental assumption we carry as a result of these biases. 

True that plants haven't built communication technology but as stated earlier, the real question is do they relly need to ? Almost all our progress as a species has been a consequence of our instinct of survival over the ages. The evolution helped us bake this progerss into our skills that we unknowingly carry over generations. So is communication by virtue of which we can transfer the information from one group to another and from one generation to another. Plants, pehaps they don't need to communicate in the first place because probably their survival isn't endangered by absence of communication. Therefore why build something you don't need ? Practical, isn't it ?

As a matter of fact, plants do communicate but probably not the way we perceive communication is. Forests have this wide network of roots under the soil called [wood wide web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycorrhizal_network) which helps plants communicate about threats like wild fire. This web is not just communication channel but also acts a supply chain manangement network. When smaller plants can't receive sunlight and nutrients due to excessively large plants blocking sunlight in dense forests, the mature plants use the same network to send vital nutrients to the younger ones. 

*On the contrary it is interesting to wander that we humans haven't been able to solve the problem of hunger despite having built most advanced technology on the planet.*  

Let's talk about a more fundamental skill than a derived one like communication i.e. *making your own food*.

We have surely learned how to make different cuisines over ages to suit varied climates, cultures and ages. We also built advanced tools to automate a large chunk of the overall process of making food leading to an overall highly efficient execution. 

However when it comes to plants, they relatively have simpler and efficient process as a whole. They don't have to cook food at all before consuming it. They can simply absorb the sunlight which is the most easiest way to consume food. They also directly consume the energy from the source itself rather than many species like ours who is dependent on the food chain which means multiple species have to process the sunlight before us and then we can consume it. In the worst case if the food chain is interrupted it can even lead to our extinction. Surely plants are also not immnue to threat of extinction but relatively we are more exposed. It is pretty evident that evolution has defnitely worked in their favour but they have developed more efficient, robust and highly secure  way of making food and we definitely haven't adapted it very well with all the technological support either.  

## Conclusion
Paning out, it seems there are vast number of such standards across which we can compare the all the species out there in contrast to the limited aspects we discussed above. Some may argue that we chose to explore only the ones where we could portray the alternative species as the superior ones, but that's not true. While pursuing the comparative study it becomes necesary to include the aspects which might not even be considering. 

The real intention is to demonstrate that the **self sustianed bias** can be shattered to seek the deeper truth which can possibly inspire the **sustained evolution and innovation**.
