The lost postcard

Ishan Ghorela

February 8, 2026 - 4 minutes read - 833 words

It was the October of 2025 when my wife and I were exploring the beaches and islands of Krabi. Needless to say this was the most relaxing place we had ever been to. The ocean waves put you in a trance. When needed you can also quickly find yourself onto a wavy ocean ride taking you to the pristine islands with breathtaking sceneries or a quick snorkelling dive into the ocean where you get to witness the most beautiful flaura and fauna.

The fun starts out of the ocean in the neighbourhood once it has gulped down the red hot sun after an evening full of colors. The entire town puts on a party vibe with lights, music and delicious food. It’s totally your choice if you want to find yourself in a fancy beachside restaurant enjoying a tropical recipe or eating a delicious sandwich while sipping the beer you just grabbed from a 24*7 outlet, though I’d recomend experiencing both because why not. Honesly, the place greets you with everything you can ask for. Surrender yourself and let it surprise you at every step.

Once we slid into the projectile of joy and serenity we couldn’t even notice how the time slipped by and we found ourselves shopping for souvenirs. That’s when we come across a bunch of postcards and they told us if we put the stamps it would actuallly get delivered to our home all the way across the ocean in India. Getting yourself a postcard as a souvenir is an act but having it delivered to your home on it’s own as a token of your visit sounded like a journey. This immediately stuck the chord with us and we found ourselves looking for the most beautiful one.

Within no time we put the stamp and wrote a beautiful message addressing ourselves. The sun had started settling down and we were ready to go for a beautiful walk on the beach. So we finally headed towards the postbox to drop our card. It was so exciting to anticpate the feeling of receiving this postcard when it would reach our home. We had already started imagining the scenario in our heads. The postbox was a fews steps away. There was already a sense of bonding with the postcard probably because we held it with so many emotions out of which I guess hope dominated the most. The emotions peaked so much that when we were there there it almost felt like letting go something that belonged to us. Maybe it was because we found something in a foreign land that we could call ours and shared a bond with it. But it was the moment, so we put the postcard in the slot, droppped it and I could almost hear the sound of it dropping on the stack. The dropping sound almost came like it saying that I am already on my way

This was it. There way no way we could get it back other than waiting for it to reach us all the way across the ocean at our home. A moment ago we had the postcard in my hand and now we had the hope in our hearts. We left the spot and headed towards the beach for one last time before we catch our flight for home the next day. Breathed in the ocean breeze one last time, enjoyed the fire show one more time, had a good dinner and roamed all over the place till 3am in the night as if we didn’t want our last walk to end. It was as if we wanted to absorb most of it before we could let it go finally, like saying goodbye probably because it makes letting go relatively easy.

It’s been 6 months since we came back and the postcard never reached us. We would often talk about where would it be now. Was it even taken out of the postbox ? Given the world has moved to the digital space, does anyone even care about the postbox anymore ? Was it sent to wrong country or an incorrect shipment ? Where would it be because it would be somewhere but not at its destination.

The fate of the postcard remains uknonwn. And then I think if you hold onto the hope the questions are endless but in the end or as Irfan Khan says it more accurately I suppose in the end, the whole of life becomes an act of letting go, but what always hurts the most is not taking a moment to say goodbye

We had our time to say goodbye to the place but we hoped to get the postcard back so we never said it goodbye. We had hope. Sometimes hope can be all the way too much to expect from the universe that reinvents itself every moment by breaking apart and integrating again, the universe that has no concept of permanence because it’s just a subjective experience of human consiousness
