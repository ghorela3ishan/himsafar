Are humans really the most intelligent species ?

Ishan Ghorela

December 20, 2025 - 3 minutes read - 531 words

Introduction

So there is this widely considered notion amongst humans that we are the most intelligent species on this planet. If someones goes upto the extent of validating and question the reasoning, many of us would come up with achievemtens like humans are the only species that came up with the idea of language, society, harnessing resources on the planet, technological innovation etc.

The Notion

If we pursue the notion this does make sense. Are there any other species who has been able to built trains, or communication technology ? Did any species learn how to cook their food ? Did they try to understand what lies outside our planet ? Did they ever try to figure out what’s inside them ? Did they pursue spirituality ?

We humans have always been curious and adaptive. We adapted to our environments, inculcated resourcefulness and harnessed surroundings around us to our leverage and progressed to the stage where we are now. We have developed language to communicate. We have been able to reach mars, moon and sent Voyager to explore the vast limits of the universe. We are building quantum computers to harness parallel universes.

Which species can beat this ?

We surely are the epitome of intelligence on this planet!

What about the questions ?

Has any other species challenged our claims so far or it’s just us who shouted the in the [echo chamber] and stamped the assumption as the fact ? Let’s talk about the plants. It’s evident they haven’t developed technology or communication. But do they really need to ? If we really need to find the absolute truth, we would have to get rid of our [internal biases]. We will need to drop the criteria we think is most suited to compare two species and try to question every fundamental assumption we carry as a result of these biases.

True that plants haven’t built communication technology but the real question is do they relly need to ? Almost all out progress has been a result of instinct of survival over the ages and evolution helped us bake this progerss into our skills that we unknowingly carry over generations. So is communication so that we can transfer the information from one group to another and from one generation to another. Plants, pehaps they don’t need to communicate in the first place because probably their survival isn’t endangered by absence of communication.

As a matter of fact plants do communicate under the soil. Forests have this wide network of roots under the soil called [wood wide web] which helps plants communicate of threats like wild fire. This web is not just communication channel but also acts a supply chain manangement network. When smaller plants can’t receive sunlights and nutrients due to excessively large plants blocking sunlight in dense forests, the more mature plants use the same network to send vital nutrients to the younger plants. On the contrary it is interesting to wonder that we humans haven’t been able to solve the problem of hunger despite being aided with most advanced technology on the planet.

Conclusion

The intention is not to disprove our assertion but to get rid of biases
